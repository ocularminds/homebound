# HomeBound: Fire TV narration

Record only the paragraphs below, in order, at a comfortable conversational pace.
Leave a short pause between paragraphs and two seconds of silence at each end.
Target: 2 minutes 20 seconds to 2 minutes 45 seconds. The screen footage will be
edited to your recording; you do not need to operate the app while speaking.
Pronunciation: Home-bound; Deh-see-oh-nis; Agent-safe; M-C-P.

---

Your television is a shared screen. But most smart-home notifications treat it
like a phone: interrupt now, show everything, and assume the right person is
watching.

HomeBound turns Fire TV into a context-aware home canvas. It understands the
viewing moment, the intended audience, and what help belongs on screen.
An Alexa-style companion adds voice to the same home experience.

Here we’re watching an original cartoon. Leo has a homework reminder waiting,
but HomeBound keeps the program clear. During the commercial, his note appears
beside the video. We can pause to read, then return to exactly where the
program stopped.

Now a guest joins. The personal note disappears. This happens on the server,
before the screen receives its data. A private message is not simply hidden
behind a card that another viewer could reveal.

The canvas can also help with what we’re watching. In this cooking scene, I ask,
“What are they cooking?” Amazon Bedrock interprets the scene and connects the
recipe to our demo pantry. Olive oil is missing. When we restock it, the recipe
updates. Shopping suggestions stay in a draft cart; no purchase is made.

Or, in a movie scene: “I like that jacket.” HomeBound brings up a similar item
from our demo catalog. “Add that to my cart” creates a draft to review, without
interrupting the viewing experience or pretending a payment happened.

Next: “I want to read a book, but keep the game on.” HomeBound previews the game
with captions, muted audio, and a room setup for reading. “Restore the room”
brings the previous preview back. Physical lights and television settings are
not being changed in this prototype.

The complementary Alexa-style screen can leave a note for Mom, and it appears
on the television when Mom is the eligible viewer. Both surfaces use the same
home state; Bedrock handles the conversational interpretation.

Behind the experience, Bedrock proposes the next interaction. Bounded context,
media, and shopping components choose what the screen may display. The model
cannot replace the audience rules or turn a shopping suggestion into a purchase.

The television canvas is our primary experience. Household presence and media
signals are labelled simulations, and Alexa+ partner access is not connected.
The demo uses our own media and catalog, with real Bedrock interpretation.

HomeBound brings the living room, voice, and home context together: useful at
the right moment, private for the right audience, and governed before action.
